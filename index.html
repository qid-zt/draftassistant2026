Every ranking here is your board, untouched: players come off in descending value, with pts/wk breaking ties between equal dollar figures. Nothing is adjusted for position, scarcity, or draft-day market behavior. Gone-by-your-pick odds and the dollar edge on recommendations come from 300 simulated drafts run at every pick, where each simulated team takes near the top of your board with a little randomness. Roster needs are the only thing that shapes what gets recommended to you. Market watch is the one exception, and it is walled off: it runs a separate model that discounts quarterbacks in one-QB leagues, promotes them in superflex, pays a premium for the top two tight ends, and reacts to position runs — purely to flag where real drafters tend to diverge from a value board. It never reorders anything else. ESPN's overall rank, positional rank and bye week from the Sept 2 PPR Top 300 are shown next to every player as a second opinion — all 172 of your players matched. They are display only: nothing sorts, scores, simulates or recommends off them. The ESPN 300 tab lists all 300 in ESPN's order, including the 128 players, kickers and defenses your board doesn't carry, so you can mark them gone when they're picked. Those players are draftable but never ranked, never simulated and never recommended — they sit at the bottom of your lineup and count only toward the pick clock. Tap any player name to open his sheet: your own notes (saved in this browser, marked with an amber dot in the pool), four outbound searches, and headlines from the last two weeks fetched live through /api/news, which reads Google News and caches for five minutes. Headlines are titles and links only — follow the link to read the piece.
